The following are trademarks of kageroumado:

- The name **"Refrax"**
- The Refrax application icon and all visual variations of it
- The Refrax wordmark and any associated logos
- The `refrax.browser` and `refrax.website` domain names

The GPL grants you rights to the **code**. It does not grant you rights
to the **brand**.

## What you may do

- Build Refrax from source for your own use
- Study, modify, and contribute back changes
- Distribute unmodified, official Refrax builds (e.g., mirror the DMG)
- Refer to Refrax by name in articles, reviews, tutorials, and similar
  factual or editorial contexts

## What requires rebranding

If you distribute a modified version of Refrax, or build a derivative
work from this source, you must:

- Choose a different name (don't call it "Refrax", "Refrax X", "Refrax
  Plus", "Refrax-NG", or anything that could be confused with Refrax)
- Replace the application icon with your own
- Make it clear, in your project description and UI, that your work is
  derived from Refrax but is not the official Refrax

This is similar to how Firefox and Chromium handle their forks
(Iceweasel, LibreWolf, Brave, etc. all use different names and icons).

## Permission requests

If you'd like to use the Refrax name or icon in a way this policy
doesn't allow, write to <mail@kagerou.glass>.

## Why this exists

The point of releasing Refrax as free software is to make the code
auditable, contributable, and survivable beyond any single maintainer.
The point of protecting the name is so that "Refrax" continues to mean
*the specific software you can trust* — not a brand that anyone can
slap on a modified build.

The two goals are complementary. You're free to fork; you're just not
free to claim the fork *is* Refrax.
